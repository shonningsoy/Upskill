# Comparison - Direct Share vs Marketplace Listing

> Direct shares are relationship-oriented; listings are product-oriented.

## Short Answer

Use a **direct share** for controlled sharing with known accounts. Use a **listing** when the dataset should be packaged as a data product with metadata, terms, discoverability, and a consumer access workflow.

## Comparison Table

| Dimension | Direct share | Marketplace/listing |
|---|---|---|
| Primary use | Known consumer account(s) | Productized distribution |
| Audience | Specific partner/internal account | Public, private, or limited consumer set |
| Metadata/terms | Minimal compared with listing | Built into listing workflow |
| Discoverability | Low | Higher, especially Marketplace |
| Operational style | Access grant | Data product management |
| Consultant recommendation | Best for simple one-to-one sharing | Best when consumers need documentation, request flow, terms, and repeatable onboarding |

## Decision Rules

- If one known partner needs one curated dataset, use a direct share first.
- If many consumers need the dataset, consider a listing.
- If legal/commercial terms, descriptions, sample queries, or usage visibility matter, a listing is usually better.
- Marketplace does not always mean fully public; listings can be private or controlled.
